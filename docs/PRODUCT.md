# Product direction

The canvas is the main workspace. The budget is a companion. The tool handles the numbers; the player owns the layout.

- Beginner-friendly plain language throughout, without a separate beginner mode or lessons.
- Start with a chosen machine tier/count at full speed; derive its rate. Support multiple finished outputs for malls and combine shared ingredient demand.
- Optimize for fewest machines within chosen tiers; explain unavoidable idle capacity without calling it a fault.
- User-defined external supplies: belt lanes for solids, source rates for fluids. Bus means external supply, not a second kind of belt.
- Compare direct insertion and belt transport by supply coverage, equipment and bottlenecks. Do not promise physical reach from schematic links.
- Keep connections visible, dragging reliable, and motion calm.
- Worked examples are optional. Do not replace the player's canvas without an explicit action.
- Numerical budgets and actual live flow must be distinguishable. No fake item flow to suggest production that the solver cannot support.

The original steady-state preference was followed by an explicit request for persistent belt items and inserter handling. Both models now exist and must be labeled honestly.
